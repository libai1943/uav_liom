Cooperative flight through intersecting routes and narrow passages requires UAVs to avoid obstacles and one another while reaching prescribed endpoints within a common fixed horizon. Local collision-avoidance corridors can conflict with motion limits and prevent a direct optimization from producing an executable plan. This paper develops a layered optimization framework that addresses these conflicts without extending the flight horizon. A hard-corridor formulation provides a fast initial path for compatible tasks. When it fails to yield an accepted trajectory, separate penalties on kinematic inconsistency and inter-UAV corridor violations allow intermediate trajectories to reshape the corridors. A second recovery layer responds to violations in the reconstructed motion by tightening consistency requirements and solving with exact kinematics. Endpoint control correction and exact integration produce rest-to-rest motions, which undergo complete-interval clearance and motion-limit checks. The local subproblems retain a sparse convex quadratic structure. Simulations examine centralized teams of up to 20 UAVs and include closed-loop rigid-body tracking under disturbances. In the examined winding tasks, the recovery layers obtain executable motions after the initial hard-corridor formulation fails, while the direct path handles compatible cases efficiently.
