\section{Conclusion}
This paper develops a layered solution procedure for fixed-horizon multi-UAV cooperative flight. A hard relative-corridor QP handles directly compatible local problems. Separate kinematic and relative-corridor penalties support further geometric updates when that path does not return an accepted motion. Acceptance-driven tolerance adjustment and exact finishing provide a second recovery layer. Endpoint moment correction, exact integration, and complete-interval checks produce the returned rest-to-rest motions.

The winding counterflow study identifies incompatible initial hard corridors and records accepted motions obtained by relaxed reconstruction at the same flight horizon. Independent phase-I checks confirm incompatibility of the initial hard constraints in two recovered layouts; the accepted continuous motions then establish feasibility of those physical tasks. The broader experiment records also contain nine accepted exact-finishing motions after soft-candidate checks fail. These observations demonstrate the role of both recovery layers. Hard relative corridors remain faster on ordinary compatible cases, motivating their direct entry into the planning procedure.

Team and grid studies characterize computational growth through 20 UAVs. Rigid-body simulations connect the accepted plans to bounded rotor actuation and feedback tracking under disturbances. Future work will extend the known-static-map setting to rotor downwash, uncertain perception, and flight experiments.
